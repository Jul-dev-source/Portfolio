## Overview

### Overview

**ZeeUs – Discover Amazing People & Exciting Events in Your City!**

New in town? Or simply looking to try something new and meet interesting people? ZeeUs makes it easy!

Whether it's a relaxed coffee meetup, a wild party, or just a walk in the park, ZeeUs helps you find events that match your interests and lets you see who's already joining.

- 👥 **Meet New People** – Connect with others who share your interests
- 🎉 **Create & Join Events** – From spontaneous meetups to large parties
- 📍 **See Who's Going Where** – Never go out alone again
- ✨ **Just Have Fun** – No stress, no complicated planning

No endless searching, no awkward first messages—just find an event, show up, meet people, and have a great time.

## Context

### Context

Cities offer countless meetups, parties and activities. Still, many people feel lonely, especially after a move. ZeeUs aims to close the gap between "there are events" and "I actually go." The app is built for people aged [20 to 40] who want to make new connections without getting lost in dating apps or group chats.

## Problem

### Problem

Finding events is tedious: they're scattered across social media, ticket portals and chat groups.

- Going alone is a barrier because you don't know who else will be there.
- Sending a first message to a stranger feels awkward.
- Existing apps focus either on dating or on pure ticket sales.

## Goals & Success Criteria

### Goals & success criteria

- Users find a suitable event in under [2 minutes].
- At least [80%] of test participants immediately understand who's attending an event.
- Joining an event takes a maximum of [3 taps].
- The design feels welcoming and relaxed, not like a dating tool.

## My Role

### My role

Owned the project from idea to clickable prototype: research, concept, user flows, wireframes, UI design, component library and usability testing.

## Research & Insights

### Research & insights

1. The biggest barrier isn't searching, it's arriving alone.
2. Seeing who's attending significantly lowers the threshold.
3. Spontaneous, casual formats (coffee, a walk) work better than big events.
4. Users don't want to fill out long profiles.

## Definition

### Definition

Target group: Newcomers, curious explorers, people who move to a new location and people who want to expand their social circle.

Guiding question: *How might we make it easy for people to find events and feel confident going alone?*

Focus for the first version: find an event, see who's going, join. I deliberately left out chat and payment features.

## Design Decisions

### Design decisions

1. **Show attendees directly on the event card.** The alternative was a participant list on the detail page. I chose avatars on the card because they build trust instantly.
2. **Interests instead of long profiles.** During onboarding, users only pick their interests. This saves time and reduces drop-off.
3. **No forced chat.** People connect through the event, not through direct messages. This removes the "awkward first message" barrier.
4. **Friendly, playful UI.** Warm colors and rounded shapes make the app feel like fun, not like an obligation.

## Design System & UI

### Design system & UI

I mainly used the Human Interface Guidelines (iOS UI Kit) to create the Design.

Individuell components, color tokens were created directly from me in Figma to fulfill the needs. This ensures consistency and would speed up the developer handoff.

## Final Solution

### Final solution

The app consists of four core areas: Discover (feed with filters), Event Detail (description, location, attendees), Create Event and Profile. The full flow from opening the app to joining an event takes just a few steps.

## Learnings

### Learnings

- The barrier users name isn't always the real one. Only the interviews showed that the issue was being alone, not searching.
- Fewer features make the product clearer. Next time I'll test earlier with low-fidelity designs.
- Presenting lo-fi Wireframes for the concept clarification is way more efficient than doing it with fine design.

## Next Steps

### Next steps

- Test with a larger group in a real city.
- Design moderation and safety features (reporting, verification).
- Design notifications and reminders.
- Explore business models (e.g. organizer features).
